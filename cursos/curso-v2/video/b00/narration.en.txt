This course comes from a software design competition run by O'Reilly, the Architecture Katas. It is presented by Neal Ford and Mark Richards, authors of the book Fundamentals of Software Architecture. A jury led by Jacqui Read picks the winner of each edition. Each edition presents the case of a business, and small teams design the solution in a short time. They hand in requirements, diagrams and written decisions. A catalog called TheKataLog gathers the winners' repositories, and this course uses twelve of them.

One of those winners designed ClearView, the platform of a nonprofit organization that lives on donations. A person looking for work uploads their résumé. The system strips out everything that identifies them and writes, from it, an anonymous story of what they can do. A company posts a job, and the system suggests which stories fit it. The company sees only the story. If it is interested, it unlocks the match, and only then does the full résumé travel to that company's human resources system. This is what the system the team designed looks like. On the left, the people. In the center, several services that share a single database. On the right, the artificial intelligence models, which do not belong to ClearView, and the companies' human resources systems, about a hundred different ones.

Every piece of this drawing is a decision, and none of them shows at first sight. Why several services share one database, when the usual thing is to give each one its own. Why the matching service cannot read the résumé, when it is one step away. Why artificial intelligence does not decide who fits which job, when that is what it is for. And what happens when one of those hundred human resources systems does not answer. The course answers those questions with what the winners left in writing. At the end, this same drawing comes back, and every piece will have its reason.
